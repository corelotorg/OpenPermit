The open regulatory interface underneath the portals.

OpenPermit is the reference implementation of Open Regulatory Infrastructure (ORI): a permissionless, vendor-neutral, machine-readable substrate for governments, practitioners, software and models to inventory, publish, traverse, verify, measure, challenge and govern regulatory processes.

Regulation as addressable evidence and graph.

A permit is one traversal through regulatory infrastructure, not the infrastructure itself. ORI preserves source-native meaning, explicit authority, jurisdiction, effective time, evidence, decisions and challenges.

Inventory

Know what applies

Jurisdictions, approvals, authorities, requirements, fees, deadlines, reviewers, evidence, exceptions and appeal paths become addressable objects.

Graph

Know what depends on what

Typed relationships expose prerequisites, parallel branches, blocking nodes, duplication, supersession and—where the slice is acyclic—topological order and critical path.

Challenge

Know what a conclusion rests on

Assertions, mappings and verification results remain attributable and challengeable. Counter-evidence and dispositions are durable graph objects, not erased comments.

Evidence

Portable proof

Documents, models, measurements, geospatial anchors, credential attestations, inspection artifacts and test reports can support requirements without becoming authority themselves.

Interfaces

Models are first-class clients

MCP is the initial reference model transport. REST, JSON-LD, graph traversal, static documentation and conformance fixtures expose the same canonical semantics.

Authority

Automation does not invent law

A passing validator proves declared checks passed. Legal approval requires an independently addressable authorized decision when applicable law or process requires one.

A concrete federal implementation target.

The first public ORI profile converts HUD's 2026 State and Local Best Practices for Home Construction into computable controls, evidence and measurements. It is federal guidance—not silently promoted into binding local law and not an endorsement of OpenPermit.

Inspect it. Test it. Implement it independently.

The public corpus is designed to be crawlable, citable and runnable. The standard is not the website and no proprietary runtime is the source of truth.

Licensed model-code and third-party standards text is not republished without rights. ORI stores citations, adoption/version data, mappings, lawful test definitions, evidence requirements, results and deltas while preserving the authoritative source.

Open at every seam.

Jurisdictions, agencies, standards bodies, code officials, builders, engineers, inspectors, universities, civic-tech maintainers, cloud providers, AI/model providers, permitting vendors, material and manufacturing suppliers, and specialist practitioners can contribute profiles, mappings, validators, evidence formats and compute capabilities. Participation is not permissioned by OpenPermit. Outside validators are not trusted merely because they register; provenance and conformance remain inspectable.
