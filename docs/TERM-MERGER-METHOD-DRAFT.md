# Term merger study: free names, cited terms, and code expression (DRAFT)

Status: **DRAFT**, 2026-09-28. Prepared for the ORI project.
Licence of this document: CC0.

> **This is analysis to inform counsel, not a legal conclusion.** It measures how ORI's words
> relate to the code text and to ordinary English. It does not decide what copyright law
> protects. The categories, thresholds and results are ORI's working estimates. Counsel should
> review them before anything built on them is published.

Tool: `verification/ori_cl/term_merger.py`. Tests: `verification/tests/test_term_merger.py`.
Results: `research/data/ori-cl-term-merger-DRAFT.json`. Evidence corpora (manifest):
`research/data/ori-cl-open-corpora-DRAFT.json`. Wikipedia phrase counts:
`research/data/ori-cl-term-open-corpus-counts-DRAFT.json`.

## 1. The question

Project principle (2026-09-28): *you cannot coin the term Apple, because an apple is an apple.*
Plain names for physical things (riser height, guard, smoke alarm, sink) cannot be owned. ORI
should not invent awkward substitutes for them. What is protected is the code's expression:

- the structure of its sentences;
- their order;
- the choice of one phrasing among many possible ones.

The idea behind this study is the merger idea. Where there is essentially only one practical
way to name a thing, the name goes with the thing. Where there are many ways to say
something, the code's particular choice is expression, and ORI must say it its own way. The
study turns that idea into measurements. Counsel decides whether and how the doctrine applies.

## 2. Categories

Every ORI-CL vocabulary term and every clause of every generated paraphrase goes into one
category:

| Category | Meaning | What ORI does |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) free name** | An ordinary name for a physical thing or its property, a unit, or a plain English word. | Uses it as is, showing evidence of ordinary use: general dictionary, open corpora, IFC/QUDT/NIST names. |
| **(b) cite** | A technical term with essentially one practical expression, including terms defined in law and IFC identifiers. | Uses it with a citation: "defined at IRC R202, as adopted by VRC (R202)", the section ids where the code uses it, or the IFC 4.3 name with attribution. |
| **(c) many expressions** | A phrasing with many plausible alternatives, or a clause rather than a name. | If the same wording occurs in the code copies (flag `re-express`), ORI rewrites it. If it does not occur there, it is already ORI's own expression, and no action is needed. |

Two more flags:

- `cite`: a (b) term that is not a defined term or an IFC name. ORI should cite where the code uses it.
- `watch`: a paraphrase clause that shares a 4 or 5 word run with the code copies, including at least one word that is not free. It is below the safeguard and should be read by a person.
- `term-at-issue`: a reviewer term that is (c) and occurs in the code. It is quoted on purpose. `docs/CITATION-POLICY.md` allows the undefined term at issue in 4 words or fewer.

## 3. Signals

All signals are numbers, booleans, section ids or ORI's own words.

| Signal | How it is measured | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Code occurrences | Count of the term, or its plural, in the local VRC 2021 Chapter 2 and 3 copies. Records the section ids. | Is the wording shared with the code at all? |
| Shared runs | The longest word run a clause shares with the code copies, and how many of its words are not free. | Paraphrase clauses; the safeguard (section 5). |
| Semantic similarity | **Used: embeddings.** `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (Apache-2.0, model card fetched from huggingface.co on 2026-09-28), run locally on the box's CPU with normalized cosine similarity. **Fallback when not installed:** WordNet Wu-Palmer similarity of aligned words, or else character-trigram cosine. The output records which backend ran. | (1) Deciding whether an alternative phrasing is plausible. (2) For each paraphrase clause, the most similar code sentence and its section id. High meaning-similarity with a short shared run is what a good paraphrase looks like. |
| Plausible alternatives | Candidate rewordings: WordNet synonyms of the whole term, and one-word substitutions. Each word's synonyms come from the one WordNet sense whose gloss best fits the term (embedding Lesk). A head noun takes only noun senses. A candidate counts if (i) its new word is common English (wordfreq Zipf ≥ 3), (ii) it differs from the original word (so 'clear' to 'clear up' does not count), (iii) the new word's similarity to the replaced word is at least 0.5, and (iv) the candidate's similarity to the term is at least 0.72 (embedding; 0.80 lexical). Abbreviations (min, max, pv) are never substituted. | Merger signal: 2 or fewer alternatives means essentially one practical expression. |
| Open non-ICC corpora | WordNet 3.0 lemma (Princeton WordNet licence: use, copy, modify and distribute for any purpose, with notice; license page fetched 2026-09-28). The Brown Corpus is no longer used (dropped 2026-09-28 because the NLTK data index states non-commercial use); phrase counts come from the licensed evidence corpora (section 3a). Wikipedia exact-phrase hit count from the public search API (counts only; cached in the open-corpus file). IFC 4.3 / QUDT / NIST / ORI Pset names recorded in the vocabulary. | Evidence of ordinary use outside the code. |
| **Public-domain and open corpora** (added 2026-09-28) | Phrase counts in the evidence corpora listed in section 3a (full build, 2026-10-03). **A phrase found in a public-domain or open-license source is strong evidence for (a)/(b).** It makes the term count as in open use, and a noun phrase that would otherwise be (c) becomes (a) (or (b) if not all its words are in the dictionary). For paraphrase clauses, the longest run shared with these sources is recorded too. Virginia locality material, and any public-domain-class source whose licence basis is not confirmed, is counted separately and is **never** strong evidence. | The main independent evidence that a name predates or exists apart from the code. |
| Physical thing | WordNet: the phrase, or its head noun, is under `physical_entity`; or the head is a measure or attribute and a modifier is physical (for example riser + height). | Separates physical-thing names from abstract phrases. |

Manufacturer data (catalogs and datasheets) was **not** used. No manufacturer pages were
fetched, and IFC Pset names stand in for industry data. Adding a manufacturer corpus is
listed as future work.

## 4. Decision procedure

The procedure is deterministic, in `classify_term`. The rules are applied in order, and the
first match wins:

1. IFC identifier → (b), with attribution to IFC 4.3.
2. Unit name → (a).
3. Keyword or relation that is a dictionary or function word → (a).
4. A multi-word term made only of defined-term names (for example the reviewer term "approved noncombustible material") → (b). Each definition is cited.
5. Defined term or a label equal to one:
   - (a) if it is a dictionary noun phrase that names a thing (WordNet lemma or physical) and has open-corpus use; the law also defines it, so R202 is cited when the legal meaning matters;
   - otherwise (b).
6. A dictionary noun phrase of 3 or fewer content words that names a physical thing or its property and has open-corpus use (WordNet lemma, 25 or more Wikipedia hits, a phrase hit in a public-domain or open evidence corpus, or a standard-data name) → (a), **whatever the number of alternatives.** A plain name stays free even if a synonym exists (smoke alarm or smoke detector; sleeping room or bedroom).
7. A dictionary noun phrase with open use and 2 or fewer alternatives → (a).
8. A dictionary noun phrase with 2 or fewer alternatives that occurs in the code but has no open-use evidence → (b), `cite`.
9. A noun phrase with 2 or fewer alternatives that does not occur in the code → (a) (ORI's plain descriptive name).
10. Otherwise → (c). It is flagged `re-express` if it occurs in the code.

A "noun phrase" here has 4 words or fewer and none of: is, are, be, where, when, unless, if,
not, or, than, shall, must, may.

**Paraphrase clauses** (`classify_clause`):

- A clause made only of free terms, numbers, units and function words is (a), or (b) if it contains a (b) term.
- Anything else is (c), because it is ORI's composed wording.
- It is flagged `re-express` if it shares any run of 6 or more words with the code copies (strict rule, section 5), and `watch` as defined in section 2.

## 5. The 6-word-run safeguard: strict is enforced, refined is information only

**Project decision (2026-09-28): the STRICT 6-word check is the enforced rule everywhere. No
counsel review of a relaxed rule is planned.** Any run of 6 or more words that an ORI file or
output shares with the local code copies fails, whatever words it contains. A term or clause
with such a run is flagged `re-express`.

Where it is enforced:

- `corpus_stats.assert_no_text` (linguistic study outputs) and `term_merger.assert_output_clean`
  (term merger results) fail on any shared 6-word run.
- `python -m ori_cl.term_merger scan PATH... --code-text CH2.txt CH3.txt` exits 1 on any shared
  run and prints file, line, run length and SHA-256 only (never the text).
- `verification/tests/test_strict_safeguard.py` runs that scan over every tracked and untracked
  file in the repository as part of the conformance suite. It is skipped only where the analyst's
  local code copies are absent (CI), because the copies are never committed.
- `classify_term` / `classify_clause` mark `re-express` on any shared 6-word run
  (`signals.strict_shared_run`).

**The refined classification** (`term_merger.shared_runs(..., free_terms)`) is kept as an
**informational report only, never a gate**. For each strict hit it says whether the run is made
only of category (a)/(b) terms, numbers, units, function words and official names
(`refined_flag_info` / `refined_run_flag_info`), with the 12-word arrangement limit. It helps a
reviewer decide how to re-express a hit; it never lets a hit pass.

**Repository status after the switch** (2026-09-28, strict scan against the Chapter 2 and 3
copies): the scan found 43 shared runs, all in files predating this study, plus one in the
US Code licence copy. All were re-expressed:

- agency names written in full were shortened to "Virginia DHCD";
- the USBC title in `docs/CITATION-POLICY.md` was reworded;
- a Chapter 3 section heading used as a unit title, two measurement glosses, a utilities summary
  and two utility phrases in `rules/irc2021/ch03/` were reworded and the units and review pack
  regenerated;
- two flood-zone phrases and one stair-landing phrase in interpretation summaries were reworded;
- the `research/licenses/usc-17-304.txt` copy was trimmed to the section text and source credits
  (the House Report notes that followed on the web page are omitted).

The strict scan now finds **0** shared runs. The repository lint for quoted shall-register text
(`conformance/validate.py`) is unchanged and still runs everywhere, including CI.

## 6. What is stored

- **Stored:** counts, section ids, SHA-256 digests of the inputs and of shared runs, and ORI's own words. ORI's words are vocabulary terms, paraphrase clauses (already in the repository), reviewer terms of 4 words or fewer, and WordNet-derived alternative phrasings.
- **Never stored:** code sentences, headings, definitions or n-grams from the code.
- The code copies (UpCodes rendering of VRC 2021 Chapters 2 and 3, a secondary source) stay on the analyst's box, outside the repository.
- The Wikipedia cache holds phrase to hit count only.

## 9. Limits

- **Word senses.** WordNet sense choice is automatic, and it errs. For example, it may pick the aircraft sense of "plane" in "roof plane", or the fabric sense of "material". Alternative counts are signals, not verdicts, and every flagged item needs a person.
- **Thresholds.** The thresholds (0.72 similarity, 2 alternatives, 25 Wikipedia hits, 12-word arrangement) are ORI's choices. They are not calibrated against any legal standard or against human judgments. Two human reviewers should score a sample before the numbers are relied on.
- **Wikipedia counts.** Some pages may themselves quote codes, so counts are evidence of circulation, not proof of independent coinage. The search API rate-limited the run (HTTP 429). Only 110 of 189 phrases were counted; the rest are null, not zero.
- **Corpora.** The full build holds 111 text sources (about 7.4 million words): federal trade manuals, federal guidance, pre-1931 books and Virginia locality documents (section 3a). It is smaller than the tens of millions of words first estimated: osha.gov, fema.gov and huduser.gov refused automated requests, www.loc.gov answered with a Cloudflare challenge, and HathiTrust's robots.txt disallows full text; none of these were bypassed. OCR noise lowers counts, older texts use older words, and a single hit in one source can move a term (see section 8). A missing hit is not evidence of code coinage.
- **Licence review.** Federal authorship is detected from item metadata and the first 5,000 words of text; automatic copyright or contractor flags were reviewed by hand and recorded in `license_review` or `license_note`. Sources still unconfirmed (contractor-prepared or reprinting third-party tables) count only as weak evidence.
- **Coverage.** Only Chapters 2 and 3 were compared, from a secondary copy (UpCodes). Other chapters, the primary ICC text and the 13VAC5-63 amendments were not.
- **Identifiers.** Identifiers (snake_case fact names, ORI Pset names) are outside the study, except where they appear as labels.
- **Not a legal test.** The categories model the merger idea. They do not decide originality, scènes à faire, fair use, or the effect of adoption into law. Those are for counsel.
