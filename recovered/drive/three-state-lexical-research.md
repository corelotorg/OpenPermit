## Research hypothesis recovered

The source proposes comparing permitting language across Virginia, Washington, Texas, and Florida to test whether action-oriented regulatory language correlates with faster process execution.

It distinguishes:

- Washington as an “environmental-technical” vocabulary;
- Texas as a “transactional-spatial” vocabulary;
- Virginia as more administrative/noun-oriented;
- Florida as a timeline/performance-oriented comparison.

## “Semantic Performance Bridge” concept

The historical proposal says the system should ingest local regulatory terms, map them to functional action semantics, and separately attach temporal/performance goals.

This is useful for ORI if separated into distinct objects:

1. **SourceTerm** — exact jurisdiction-native expression.
2. **SourceAuthority** — law, regulation, form, policy, code, or agency source.
3. **Concept** — normalized semantic concept, if justified.
4. **Action** — executable or observable regulatory action.
5. **MappingAssertion** — source term → normalized concept/action, with mapper, evidence, confidence, version, and challenge status.
6. **TemporalConstraint** — deadline, review window, tolling rule, or service-level target, sourced independently.
7. **PerformanceObservation** — measured outcome; never inferred from vocabulary alone.

That separation prevents an attractive linguistic hypothesis from becoming an unsupported legal assertion.

## ORI disposition

This artifact resolves an important historical question: **Washington/Texas cross-jurisdiction lexical work did exist.** It also confirms the earlier direction toward verb/action-oriented semantic crosswalks.

It does **not** yet establish a complete Washington or Texas regulatory inventory, and it does **not** validate the source’s performance claims. Before freezing ORI vocabulary:

- verify each cited statute/current version from primary sources;
- inventory actual forms, rules, agencies, decisions, and process edges;
- preserve source-native terms exactly;
- model mappings as challengeable assertions rather than replacements;
- test whether proposed action concepts improve interoperability without erasing legal distinctions;
- measure process latency separately from language choice.
